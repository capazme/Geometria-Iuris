Robustness & caveats

Three anchor results carry the chapter (see Home). Seven smaller investigations either strengthen those results or qualify them; this page houses all seven, alongside a methodological reframing of the headline number — the Y caveat — that the rest of the dashboard refers back to.

The opening half (D, G, H) shows that the cross-tradition gap survives pool perturbation, that the divergence operates at the level of individual same-lemma terms, and that the empirical K threshold for attestation is grounded. The closing half (F, X, Y, Z) discloses limits: stratification by k-NN confidence, dual injection of control terms, the reframing of the legal signal as the attested-bare gap, and the partial failure of a naive tier hierarchy.

D · Δρ_sym stability under background injection

Legal scenarioHow robust is the gap if the lexicon is partly contaminated with background legal terms — terms that the curator did not vet by hand, but that the corpus does include?
Result in wordsThe cross-tradition gap Δρ_sym attested moves from 0.538 at 0% background to 0.590 at 75% background. The signal does not collapse — it rises slightly. The pattern is structural.

Mean ± 95% CI across 10 pool replicates per p_bg level. Baseline (p_bg = 0) matches the headline Δρ_sym attested.

Take-homePool curation moves the absolute number slightly but does not erase the cross-tradition gap. Chapter 4 may cite the headline Δρ_sym attested with confidence: the gap persists regardless of which background terms are added to the pool.
Technical apparatus
Δρsym(p) = (ρ̄W(p) + ρ̄S(p)) / 2 − ρ̄cross(p)

Δρ at 0% = 0.538  ·  Δρ at 25% = 0.542  ·  Δρ at 75% = 0.590  ·  replicates / level = 10

Pool replicates over background subsamples; CI is across replicates, not within a single pool (no Mantel inside this curve).

G · Same-lemma divergence (term-level proof)

Legal scenarioConsider a term like specimen signature in English law and its direct Chinese translation 簽名樣本 (literally signature sample): both designate the same legal object, a reference exemplar of an authorised signer's handwriting deposited with a bank or court. If we ask two separate models, each trained on a single language tradition, to encode the pair, do they recognise the two strings as the same concept? And if we ask a single model that encodes both languages simultaneously, does it agree? The question matters: if the two encoders disagree but a single bilingual model agrees, the disagreement cannot be an encoder calibration artefact. It must lie in what each language-specialised encoder has absorbed from its own tradition.
Result in wordsOn the same set of same-lemma legal pairs, two encoders trained one on English legal corpora and one on Chinese legal corpora (BGE-EN-large × BGE-ZH-large) report cosine similarity in the band −0.11 to +0.10: at best mild alignment, often outright anti-correlation. A single bilingual encoder that places both languages in one semantic space (BGE-M3-EN × BGE-M3-ZH) reports cosine in the band +0.27 to +0.87 on the very same pairs. The disagreement is not a property of the encoder architectures: when the tradition layer is removed by a shared encoder, the lemmas align.

Each point is one same-lemma pair (English headword paired with its canonical Chinese translation in the HK DOJ bilingual glossary). The x-axis is the cosine under the two tradition-specialised encoders; the y-axis is the cosine under the single bilingual encoder. The cloud sits consistently above the y = x diagonal: bilingual cosine exceeds cross-encoder cosine on essentially every pair.

Take-homeCross-tradition divergence on the very same legal lemma is a property of how each tradition's language and corpus encode the concept, not a calibration mismatch between encoder architectures. When both languages flow through a single model, the divergence resolves. Chapter 4 should cite G as the term-level proof of the headline RSA claim: the encoder is a witness of the tradition, and the divergence the witness reports is doctrinal, not technical.
Technical apparatus
cos(en, zh) = (emben · embzh) / (‖emben‖ × ‖embzh‖) on L2-normalised pooled vectors

eligible same-lemma pairs = 4 156  ·  min K filter = K ≥ 2 attestations per term, each language  ·  tradition-specialised encoders = BGE-EN-large × BGE-ZH-large  ·  bilingual encoder = BGE-M3-EN × BGE-M3-ZH  ·  cross-tradition cos range = [−0.106, +0.100]  ·  bilingual cos range = [+0.273, +0.867]

Same-lemma pairs are English headwords paired with their canonical Chinese translation in the Hong Kong DOJ bilingual glossary, restricted to terms with at least two ordinance attestations in each language. Cosine is computed on the pooled mean of attested context embeddings. No Mantel test is required on this slice: the claim is a contrast between two encodings of the same term set.

H · K saturation: why K ≥ 4?

Legal scenarioEach attested embedding for a term is the mean of K real ordinance contexts. If K is too small, the representation is dominated by noise; if K is large, it is dominated by the corpus statistic. What K makes the cross-tradition signal stable?
Result in wordsAt K = 1 the cross-tradition ρ̄ is negative (−0.13) — the signal is below noise. At K = 4 it reaches the saturation band; it climbs modestly through K = 8 (+0.22). The K ≥ 4 threshold used throughout the chapter is empirically justified.

ρ̄_cross attested on a single bilingual comparison (BGE-EN-large × BGE-ZH-large) with the background pool partitioned by K_min. The K ≥ 4 band frames the operating regime adopted in §2.3.

Take-homeK ≥ 4 is not a convention: at lower K the cross-tradition signal is anti-correlated with itself across pool replicates. Chapter 2 §2.3 cites H as the operational justification for the threshold.
Technical apparatus

ρ̄_cross at K=1 = -0.132  ·  ρ̄_cross at K=4-7 = +0.149  ·  ρ̄_cross at K=8 = +0.218  ·  core reference = 0.246

All buckets evaluated on the same encoder pair; only the bg subsample varies. n_pairs = 1 per bucket (single encoder pair).

Y · The legal signal is the gap, not the absolute

Legal scenarioThe headline Δρ_sym attested = 0.543 describes the cross-tradition gap in the attested encoding of the legal lexicon. Is this gap a property of legal meaning, or is part of it already present in the encoders before any ordinance context is added? To isolate the legal contribution, the experiment computes a methodological baseline: the same Δρ_sym on the bare encoding (lemma in isolation, no context), both on the 364 core terms and on 100 non-legal control words (pronouns, deixis, common nouns).
Result in wordsThe bare baseline on the core returns 0.165: an encoder-tradition signal with no legal content. The same baseline computed on 100 non-legal control words returns 0.156, statistically indistinguishable from the core baseline, confirming that the bare gap is shaped by the encoders, not by the legal vocabulary. The legal signal is the contribution that attestation adds on top: 0.378 = 0.543 − 0.165.

F · Confidence-stratified injection

Legal scenarioWhen background terms are injected into the pool, do high-confidence ones — clearly belonging to a single legal domain — boost the signal more than low-confidence ones, or less?
Result in wordsLow-confidence (semantically ambiguous) background increases the signal slightly; high-confidence (categorical) background suppresses it slightly. Both shifts are within ± 0.03, with overlapping CIs. Interpret as a hint, not a finding.

Baseline = core-only. Injection strata n_inject = 91, n_replicates = 20. Error bars are 95% CI across replicates.

Take-homeCounter-intuitively, the signal sits at the boundary of the pool, not in its centre. Effect size is small; Chapter 4 may cite F as an interpretive hint but not as evidence.

X · Dual robustness — injection of control terms

Legal scenarioD injected legal background terms (still legal, just un-curated). What happens if we inject genuinely non-legal vocabulary into the bare pool?
Result in wordsΔρ_sym bare drops monotonically from 0.246 at 0% control to 0.222 at the upper limit. The direction is right; the magnitude is small because the baseline itself is small.

Dual of D, computed on bare encodings only (controls have no HK ordinance attestation). 15 replicates per level.

Take-homeBare Δρ_sym is sensitive to pool contamination but only weakly. Chapter 4 cites D and X together as a paired robustness statement: attested is stable upward, bare decays downward.

Open technical detail

Δρ_sym at 0% control = 0.246  ·  Δρ_sym at upper limit = 0.222  ·  replicates / level = 15

Control pool capped at 100; the largest p_control actually reachable is approximately 27%.

Z · The naive tier hierarchy does not hold

Legal scenarioIntuition says: core terms should cluster more tightly than background terms, which in turn should cluster more tightly than the everyday-language control. The corpus pipeline was designed under that expectation. Does the geometry agree?
Result in wordsOnly 3 of 10 models satisfy the monotonic hierarchy median(core-core) < median(core-bg) < median(core-control). The other models place background terms farther from core than the control vocabulary. Tier classification is a curatorial property of the corpus, not a geometric one.

Per-model median cosine distance on the three pair populations. ✓ marks models with monotonic hierarchy.

Take-homeBackground and control occupy distinct, model-dependent regions of the embedding. Chapter 4 §4.2 should disclose Z as an honest limit: the corpus tiers are operational, not semantic.
Technical apparatus
Open technical detail

n models = 10  ·  monotonic = 3 / 10  ·  test = Mann-Whitney on cosine medians

Distances computed on bare encodings (controls are bare-only). Hierarchy boolean as reported by ext_Z_tier_hierarchy.

A · k-NN domain assignment of the 9 045 background terms

The k-NN assigner labels each background term with the legal domain voted by its seven nearest core neighbours. Mean assignment confidence is 0.515, with a long-tailed distribution biased toward procedure and criminal. The output supports F's confidence stratification, but is itself a reusable resource for future pool expansion.

E · Out-of-sample coherence of the 6 axes on background

The six Kozlowski axes constructed on the curated 364-term pool were projected onto the 9 045 background terms (attested). Within-domain mean / std are stable for the three pool-robust axes (individual_collective, public_private, natural_positive) and noisier for the three pool-sensitive ones (rights_duties, status_contract, state_market). The pool-sensitivity flag of §3.2.4 generalises beyond the curated pool.
